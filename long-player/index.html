Privacy Policy

Long Player

Last updated 10 September 2026

Long Player collects no personal data whatsoever. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDK of any kind. Nothing you do in the app is sent to me or to anyone else.

What the app reads

Long Player asks permission to read your Apple Music library. It uses that to work out which albums in your library are complete — every track present — and to group and rank them by release year. It reads album titles, artist names, track titles, track numbers, release dates and download status.

All of that is read on your device and stays on your device. If you deny the permission, the app tells you what it needs and offers to open Settings; it does not work around the refusal.

What the app stores, and where

  • Your rankings, hidden albums and year corrections are stored in a file inside the app's own storage on your device.
  • A cache of your last library scan and downloaded album artwork, so the app opens instantly. This is a cache; iOS may clear it.
  • Your app settings — which filters are on, which sections are collapsed.

Deleting the app deletes all of it.

iCloud sync

If you turn on Sync Rankings, your rankings are stored in your own private iCloud database using Apple's CloudKit, so your devices agree with each other. This data lives in your personal iCloud account under your Apple Account.

I cannot see any of it. A private CloudKit database is accessible only to you. As the developer I have no access to its contents, and no way to identify who is using the app. Apple's handling of iCloud data is covered by the Apple Privacy Policy.

Sync is off unless you turn it on, and turning it off stops it.

Network connections

Long Player makes no connection to any server I control. It has exactly two reasons to reach the network, both to Apple:

  • Apple Music catalogue lookups (via MusicKit), to check an album's real tracklist so the app can tell a complete album from one missing a track. This needs an Apple Music subscription; without one the app falls back to what your library alone can tell it.
  • A single public iTunes lookup that trades a song's store identifier for its album identifier, so "play this album in Music" opens the right record. It carries no identifier of you, requires no key, and I never receive the request or its response.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifier, and no tracking as defined by Apple's App Tracking Transparency. The app's privacy manifest declares this.

Children

The app collects no data from anyone, of any age.
